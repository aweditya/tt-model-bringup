% =====================================================================
\begin{column}{\colwidth}

  % ------------------- Project -------------------
  \begin{block}{Project}
    End-to-end bringup of three modern LLM families on a single
    \textbf{$1\times4$ Blackhole P150} mesh: dense GQA, MoE with Gated
    DeltaNet recurrence, and a hybrid sliding/global-attention model.
    Each model walks from HF reference to a continuous-batching HTTP
    chat server, gated against a CPU fp32 oracle.
  \end{block}

  % ------------------- Hardware -------------------
  \begin{block}{Hardware}
    \begin{center}\small
    \setlength{\tabcolsep}{6pt}
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
    \begin{tabular}{l l}
      \toprule
      Mesh      & $1\times4$ Blackhole P150 (4 chips) \\
      DRAM      & 32 GB GDDR6/chip, \textbf{404 GB/s} stream \\
      On-chip   & 110 Tensix cores, 1.39 MiB L1/core \\
      Host link & 1.4 GB/s tilize-bound \\
      \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}
  \end{block}

  % ------------------- Models Brought Up -------------------
  \begin{block}{Models Brought Up}
    \begin{center}\small
    \setlength{\tabcolsep}{5pt}
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
    \begin{tabular}{l c l c}
      \toprule
      \textbf{Model} & \textbf{Params} & \textbf{Architecture} & \textbf{Status} \\
      \midrule
      Qwen3.6-27B      & 27B & Dense GQA, TP4        & shipped \\
      Qwen3.6-35B-A3B  & 35B & MoE + Gated DeltaNet  & $B{=}1$ \\
      Gemma 4 12B IT   & 12B & Hybrid sliding/global & shipped \\
      \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}
  \end{block}

  % ------------------- Methodology -------------------
  \begin{block}{Methodology}
    A staged ladder, each rung gated on cosine vs.\ a CPU fp32 oracle:
    single-layer $\to$ full forward $\to$ multi-step decode $\to$ long
    context $\to$ trace $\to$ continuous batching $\to$ HTTP. Each new
    model reused $\sim$80--90\% of the prior bringup. Validation is
    teacher-forced cosine \emph{and} MAD per layer; six of seven
    silent-failure classes are invisible at decode pos 0 alone.
  \end{block}

  % ------------------- What Mattered (moved from col 2) -------------------
  \begin{block}{What Mattered}
    Three changes account for most of the throughput delta over a
    naive port.
    \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.7ex}\setlength\parskip{0pt}
      \item \textbf{Paged SDPA, HiFi2 fp16 accumulation} --- kernel-time
            win; $+62\%$ on 27B TP single-stream.
      \item \textbf{Vocab-sharded lm\_head, on-device argmax} --- a 600
            KB host readback collapses to 8 bytes; $+8\%$ Gemma 4,
            $+5\%$ 27B.
      \item \textbf{Two-phase trace warmup} --- compile every path
            first, capture all traces back-to-back; otherwise decode
            JIT corrupts prefill trace memory.
    \end{itemize}
  \end{block}

\begin{column}{\colwidth}

  % ------------------- Correctness -------------------
  \begin{block}{Correctness}
    Three orthogonal gates, run per model on every change.

    \begin{center}\small
    \setlength{\tabcolsep}{5pt}
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
    \begin{tabular}{l c c c}
      \toprule
      \textbf{Gate} & \textbf{27B} & \textbf{35B-A3B} & \textbf{Gemma 4} \\
      \midrule
      HF cosine ladder        & PASS      & cos$_{L32}{\ge}0.987$ & PASS \\
      Multi-turn KV retention & PASS      & 3/3 PASS              & PASS \\
      Traced $=$ Eager        & PASS      & PASS                  & \textbf{100/100} \\
      Needle haystack         & $L{=}500$ & coherent              & 3/3 ($L{\le}512$) \\
      \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}

    Validation is against a CPU fp32 oracle with per-sub-op hooks.
    Cosine alone is insufficient ($\cos(a,ka){=}1$); MAD is reported at
    every probed position.
  \end{block}

  % ------------------- Continuous Batching -------------------
  \begin{block}{Continuous Batching}
    Iteration-level Orca-style scheduler: per-step admission and
    eviction, per-slot paged SDPA over a shared KV pool, slot-level
    content-keyed prefix cache. Each slot advances at its own position
    in a single batched forward.
  \end{block}

  % ------------------- Prefix Cache -------------------
  \begin{block}{Prefix Cache in Practice}
    Two-turn 27B chat over HTTP:
    \begin{center}\small
    \setlength{\tabcolsep}{8pt}
    \renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.15}
    \begin{tabular}{l r r}
      \toprule
      \textbf{Turn} & \textbf{Prompt} & \textbf{Prefill} \\
      \midrule
      1 (cold)   & 105 tok & 19.26 s \\
      2 (PC hit) & 172 tok & \textbf{3.06 s} \\
      \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}
    Turn 2 is a \textbf{6.3$\times$} prefill speedup despite a longer
    prompt: the cached KV is reused for the shared prefix and only the
    suffix is computed.
  \end{block}

  % ------------------- Conclusion -------------------
  \begin{block}{Conclusion}
    A single $1\times4$ Blackhole P150 mesh, with a disciplined bringup
    recipe, takes a modern LLM from spec to byte-stable HTTP chat at
    \textbf{316 tok/s} on 12B and \textbf{232 tok/s} on 27B. The open
    stack lets a small team bring up new architectures (MoE, hybrid
    attention) without a vendor roadmap.
  \end{block}
