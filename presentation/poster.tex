% =====================================================================
% COLUMN 1 --- Project + Architecture
% =====================================================================
\begin{column}{\colwidth}

  % ------------------- Project -------------------
  \begin{block}{Project}
    End-to-end bringup of three modern LLM families on a single
    \textbf{$1\times4$ Blackhole P150} mesh: dense GQA, MoE with Gated
    DeltaNet recurrence, and a hybrid sliding/global-attention model.
    Every model walks from HF reference to a continuous-batching HTTP
    chat server, gated end-to-end against a CPU fp32 oracle.
  \end{block}

  % ------------------- Hardware -------------------
  \begin{block}{Hardware}
    \begin{center}\small
    \begin{tabular}{l l}
      \toprule
      Mesh           & $1\times4$ Blackhole P150 (4 chips) \\
      DRAM           & 32 GB GDDR6/chip, \textbf{404 GB/s} measured stream \\
      On-chip        & 110 Tensix cores, 1.39 MiB L1/core \\
      Host link      & 1.4 GB/s tilize-bound \\
      \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}
  \end{block}

  % ------------------- Models Brought Up -------------------
  \begin{block}{Models Brought Up}
    \begin{center}\small
    \begin{tabular}{l c l c}
      \toprule
      \textbf{Model} & \textbf{Params} & \textbf{Architecture} & \textbf{Status} \\
      \midrule
      Qwen3.6-27B          & 27B  & Dense GQA, TP4                  & shipped \\
      Qwen3.6-35B-A3B      & 35B  & MoE + Gated DeltaNet ($\sim$3B active) & shipped, $B{=}1$ \\
      Gemma 4 12B IT       & 12B  & Hybrid sliding/global attention & shipped \\
      \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}

    All three reach byte-stable HTTP chat with continuous batching;
    each new model reused $\sim$80--90\% of the previous bringup's
    scaffolding (CB engine, dev harness, HF oracle, paged SDPA).
  \end{block}

  % ------------------- Methodology (collapsed) -------------------
  \begin{block}{Methodology}
    A staged ladder gates each rung on cosine vs.\ an HF fp32 oracle:
    single-layer $\to$ full forward $\to$ multi-step decode $\to$ long
    context $\to$ trace capture $\to$ continuous batching $\to$ HTTP.
    Validation is teacher-forced multi-step cosine \emph{and} MAD per
    layer; six of seven silent-failure classes are invisible at decode
    pos 0 alone. Throughout, comparison is to ground truth, never a
    sibling TT path.
  \end{block}

\end{column}

  \begin{block}{Roofline}
    Decode at $B{=}1$ is weight-bandwidth-bound:
    $t_{\min} \approx \tfrac{\text{active GB/chip}}{\text{404 GB/s}}$.

    \begin{center}\small
    \begin{tabular}{l r r r}
      \toprule
      \textbf{Model} & \textbf{Traced ms/tok} & \textbf{tok/s} & \textbf{\% ceiling ($B{=}1$)} \\
      \midrule
      Gemma 4 12B          & \textbf{47.5}  & \textbf{21.0} & 31\% of 67 tok/s \\
      Qwen3.6-27B (TP4)    & \textbf{77.0}  & \textbf{12.9} & 22\% of 60 tok/s \\
      Qwen3.6-35B-A3B      & \textbf{81.2}  & \textbf{12.3} & 4.6\% of 270 tok/s \\
      \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}

    Continuous batching closes the gap on real workloads:
    $B{=}32$ amortizes weight reads $\sim$28$\times$.
  \end{block}

  % ------------------- What Mattered -------------------
  \begin{block}{What Mattered}
    Three changes account for the majority of the throughput delta over
    a naive port.
    \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{0.4ex}
      \item \textbf{Paged SDPA with HiFi2 fp16 accumulation} ---
            kernel-time win, $+62\%$ on 27B TP single-stream.
      \item \textbf{Vocab-sharded lm\_head with on-device argmax} ---
            collapses a 600 KB host readback to 8 bytes; $+8\%$ on
            Gemma 4, $+5\%$ on 27B.
      \item \textbf{Two-phase trace warmup} --- compile all paths
            first, then capture all traces back-to-back. Without it,
            decode JIT corrupts prefill trace memory at multi-trace
            coexistence ($\to$ 99\% CPU hang).
    \end{itemize}
  \end{block}

% COLUMN 3 --- Correctness + Conclusions
% =====================================================================
\begin{column}{\colwidth}

  % ------------------- Correctness -------------------
  \begin{block}{Correctness}
    Three orthogonal gates, run per model on every change.

    \begin{center}\small
    \begin{tabular}{l c c c}
      \toprule
      \textbf{Gate} & \textbf{27B} & \textbf{35B-A3B} & \textbf{Gemma 4} \\
      \midrule
      HF cosine ladder       & PASS  & cos$_{L32}{\ge}0.987$ & PASS \\
      Multi-turn KV retention & PASS  & 3/3 PASS              & PASS \\
      Traced $=$ Eager        & PASS  & PASS                  & \textbf{100/100} \\
      Needle haystack         & $L{=}500$ & coherent          & 3/3 ($L{\le}512$) \\
      \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}

    Validation gates against a CPU fp32 oracle with per-sub-op hooks.
    Cosine alone is insufficient ($\cos(a, ka){=}1$); MAD is reported
    at every probed position.
  \end{block}

  % ------------------- Continuous Batching -------------------
  \begin{block}{Continuous Batching}
    Iteration-level Orca-style scheduler: per-step admission and
    eviction, per-slot paged SDPA over a shared KV pool, slot-level
    content-keyed prefix cache. Each slot advances at its own position
    in a single batched forward.
  \end{block}

  % ------------------- Prefix Cache -------------------
  \begin{block}{Prefix Cache in Practice}
    Two-turn 27B chat over HTTP:
    \begin{center}\small
    \begin{tabular}{l r r}
      \toprule
      \textbf{Turn} & \textbf{Prompt} & \textbf{Prefill} \\
      \midrule
      1 (cold)         & 105 tok & 19.26 s \\
      2 (PC hit)       & 172 tok & \textbf{3.06 s} \\
      \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
    \end{center}
    A prefix-cache hit on turn 2 yields a \textbf{6.3$\times$} prefill
    speedup despite the longer prompt; the new prompt re-uses the
    cached KV for the shared prefix and only computes the suffix.
  \end{block}

  % ------------------- Conclusion -------------------
  \begin{block}{Conclusion}
    A single $1\times4$ Blackhole P150 mesh, paired with a disciplined
    bringup recipe, takes a modern LLM from HF spec to byte-stable
    HTTP chat at \textbf{316 tok/s aggregate} on 12B and
    \textbf{232 tok/s} on 27B. The hardware is competitive on
    real-workload throughput; the open software stack lets a small
    team bring up new architectures (MoE recurrence, hybrid attention,
    dual-head-dim) end-to-end without waiting on a vendor roadmap.
  \end{block}

\end{column}
